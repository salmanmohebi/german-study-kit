title: sein (Präsens)
level: A1
topic_id: r-a1-01-sein
tags: [a1, sein, personalpronomen]

- Ihr {{c1::seid}} heute sehr leise. | You all are very quiet today. | **ihr → seid** is the form learners forget most. It is the only *sein* form ending in *-d*.
- {{c1::Sind}} Sie Frau Weber? | Are you Mrs Weber? | In a yes/no question the verb comes first. Formal **Sie** takes *sind*, identical to *sie* (they).
- Meine Eltern {{c1::sind}} im Urlaub. | My parents are on holiday. | A plural noun subject takes the *sie*-form **sind**, even without the pronoun.
- Das {{c1::ist}} nicht mein Koffer. | That is not my suitcase. | **nicht** goes after the conjugated verb; *sein* stays in position 2.
- Wo {{c1::bist}} du gerade? | Where are you right now? | After a question word the verb stays in position 2: *Wo — bist — du*.
- Wir {{c1::sind}} seit Montag hier. | We have been here since Monday. | German uses **present tense + seit** where English uses the present perfect.
- Ich {{c1::bin}} Ingenieur von Beruf. | I am an engineer by profession. | Professions take **no article** after *sein*: *Ich bin Arzt*, not *ein Arzt*.
- Der Kaffee {{c1::ist}} noch heiß. | The coffee is still hot. | Predicate adjectives after *sein* are **never declined**: *ist heiß*, not *ist heißer*.
- Du {{c1::bist}} nicht allein. | You are not alone. | **du → bist**; the *-st* ending marks *du* in nearly every German verb.
- Es {{c1::ist}} Viertel nach acht. | It is a quarter past eight. | Clock time and weather always use impersonal **es ist**.
- Herr Klein, {{c1::sind}} Sie müde? | Mr Klein, are you tired? | Addressing someone by name does not count as position 1 — the verb still leads the question.
- Sie {{c1::ist}} meine Schwester. | She is my sister. | *sie* + **ist** = she; *sie* + *sind* = they. The verb form disambiguates.
- Wir {{c1::sind}} alle bereit. | We are all ready. | **alle** follows the verb and refers back to *wir*.
- Ich {{c1::bin}} nicht sicher. | I am not sure. | Word order: subject – verb – *nicht* – adjective.
- Ihr {{c1::seid}} sehr willkommen. | You all are very welcome. | Plural informal *ihr* is used for a group of friends; formal groups still get **Sie sind**.
- {{c1::Ist}} das dein Fahrrad? | Is that your bicycle? | **das** as a demonstrative works for any gender: *Ist das dein Fahrrad / deine Tasche?*
