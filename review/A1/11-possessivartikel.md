---
title: Possessivartikel
level: A1
topic_id: r-a1-11-possessiv
tags: [a1, possessivartikel, kasus]
---

- {{c1::Mein}} Bruder wohnt in Zürich. | My brother lives in Zurich. | Nominative masculine possessive has **no ending**, like *ein*.
- {{c1::Meine}} Schwester studiert Medizin. | My sister studies medicine. | Feminine nominative takes **-e**: *meine Schwester*.
- Wie heißt {{c1::dein}} Hund? | What's your dog's name? | Neuter and masculine nominative are both bare: *dein Hund, dein Auto*.
- {{c1::Ihre}} Kollegen sind nett. | Her colleagues are nice. | Plural possessives always end in **-e** in the nominative and accusative.
- Ich habe {{c1::seinen}} Namen vergessen. | I've forgotten his name. | Masculine accusative → **-en**; *Name* is an n-noun, hence *Namen*.
- Wo ist {{c1::unsere}} Wohnung? | Where is our flat? | *unser* + feminine *-e* → **unsere**, keeping the *er* of the stem.
- Das ist {{c1::euer}} Problem. | That is your problem. | *euer* is bare before a neuter noun; with an ending it loses the second *e*: *euer → eure*.
- Hast du {{c1::deine}} Tickets dabei? | Do you have your tickets with you? | Plural accusative **deine**; *dabeihaben* is separable.
- Ich mag {{c1::ihr}} neues Kleid. | I like her new dress. | *ihr* (her) is bare before a neuter accusative noun; the adjective carries *-es*.
- {{c1::Sein}} Vater ist Pilot. | His father is a pilot. | *sein* = his/its; *ihr* = her/their. The owner decides, not the noun.
- Kennst du {{c1::meinen}} Chef? | Do you know my boss? | Masculine accusative **meinen** — the single most tested ending at A1.
- {{c1::Ihr}} Pass ist abgelaufen, Frau Meier. | Your passport has expired, Mrs Meier. | Capitalized **Ihr** = formal *your*, distinct from lowercase *ihr* (her/their).
- Wir verkaufen {{c1::unser}} Auto. | We are selling our car. | Neuter accusative is bare: **unser Auto**.
- {{c1::Ihre}} Kinder gehen schon zur Schule. | Their children already go to school. | *zur* = *zu der*, a fixed dative contraction.
- Ich rufe {{c1::meine}} Mutter jeden Sonntag an. | I call my mother every Sunday. | Feminine accusative **meine**; the separable *an* closes the clause.
- {{c1::Dein}} Deutsch ist wirklich besser geworden. | Your German has really got better. | Language names are neuter, so the possessive stays bare.
