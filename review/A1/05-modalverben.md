title: Modalverben
level: A1
topic_id: r-a1-05-modalverben

---

- Ich {{c1::kann}} heute nicht kommen. | I can't come today. | Modal in position 2, infinitive at the **end** — the Satzklammer.
- {{c1::Darf}} ich hier rauchen? | May I smoke here? | *dürfen* = permission; *können* = ability. Modals take **no ending** for *ich* and *er*.
- Wir {{c1::müssen}} noch einkaufen gehen. | We still have to go shopping. | Two infinitives stack at the end: *einkaufen gehen*.
- Du {{c1::sollst}} deine Tabletten nehmen. | You are supposed to take your pills. | *sollen* = someone else's instruction; *müssen* = necessity.
- Sie {{c1::will}} Ärztin werden. | She wants to become a doctor. | *wollen* → **will** for *ich/er/sie/es*. Careful: it is not English *will*.
- Ihr {{c1::könnt}} das schaffen. | You all can do it. | *ihr* form of a modal: stem + **-t**, no umlaut: *könnt*.
- Ich {{c1::möchte}} einen Kaffee, bitte. | I would like a coffee, please. | **möchten** is the polite Konjunktiv of *mögen* and needs no infinitive with a clear object.
- Er {{c1::mag}} klassische Musik. | He likes classical music. | *mögen* → **mag** expresses liking a thing, used without an infinitive.
- {{c1::Musst}} du wirklich schon gehen? | Do you really have to leave already? | *müssen* → **musst** for *du*, with a single *s* before *-t*.
- Kinder {{c1::dürfen}} hier nicht spielen. | Children are not allowed to play here. | *nicht dürfen* = prohibition. *nicht müssen* would mean "don't have to".
- Ich {{c1::kann}} kein Italienisch. | I don't speak any Italian. | With languages *können* works **without** an infinitive.
- Wir {{c1::wollen}} am Wochenende wandern. | We want to go hiking at the weekend. | Plural forms are identical to the infinitive: *wollen*.
- {{c1::Sollen}} wir ein Taxi rufen? | Shall we call a taxi? | *sollen* in a question = offering or asking for advice.
- Du {{c1::darfst}} mein Auto nehmen. | You may take my car. | *dürfen* → **darfst**; the umlaut disappears in the singular.
- Sie {{c1::müssen}} an der Ampel links abbiegen. | You have to turn left at the traffic light. | With a separable verb the whole infinitive *abbiegen* stays together at the end.
- Er {{c1::kann}} sehr gut Klavier spielen. | He can play the piano very well. | Adverbs of manner sit before the final infinitive.
