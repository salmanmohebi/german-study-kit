---
title: haben (Präsens)
level: A1
topic_id: r-a1-02-haben
tags: [a1, haben, akkusativ]
---

- Ich {{c1::habe}} keine Zeit. | I have no time. | **kein-** replaces *nicht ein*: *Ich habe keine Zeit*, never *nicht Zeit*.
- {{c1::Hast}} du einen Moment? | Do you have a moment? | *haben* takes the **accusative**: masculine *ein* → *einen*.
- Er {{c1::hat}} zwei Brüder. | He has two brothers. | **er/sie/es → hat** (no *-t* added twice; the stem loses its *b*).
- Wir {{c1::haben}} ein Problem. | We have a problem. | Neuter *ein* is unchanged in the accusative — only masculine changes.
- Ihr {{c1::habt}} Glück gehabt. | You all got lucky. | *ihr* → **habt**. The past participle of *haben* is *gehabt*.
- Die Studenten {{c1::haben}} viele Fragen. | The students have a lot of questions. | **viele** takes the plural adjective ending *-e*; no article needed.
- Meine Kollegin {{c1::hat}} morgen Geburtstag. | My colleague has her birthday tomorrow. | German says *Geburtstag haben*, not *sein Geburtstag ist*.
- {{c1::Haben}} Sie eine Reservierung? | Do you have a reservation? | Feminine *eine* stays *eine* in the accusative.
- Ich {{c1::habe}} nach dem Sport immer Durst. | I'm always thirsty after sport. | States use **haben + noun**: *Hunger/Durst/Angst haben*, not *sein*.
- Das Zimmer {{c1::hat}} kein Fenster. | The room has no window. | Neuter *kein* has no ending; masculine would be *keinen*.
- Du {{c1::hast}} recht. | You are right. | Fixed expression **recht haben** — English uses *to be*, German uses *haben*.
- Wir {{c1::haben}} noch nichts gegessen. | We haven't eaten anything yet. | *haben* also serves as the **auxiliary** for the Perfekt.
- Er {{c1::hat}} Angst vor Hunden. | He is afraid of dogs. | *Angst haben vor* + **dative** — the preposition fixes the case.
- {{c1::Habt}} ihr eure Tickets? | Do you all have your tickets? | The *ihr*-possessive *euer* becomes **eure** before a plural noun.
- Ich {{c1::habe}} den Schlüssel vergessen. | I forgot the key. | Definite masculine *der* → **den** in the accusative.
- Sie {{c1::hat}} immer gute Ideen. | She always has good ideas. | **immer** sits right after the verb, before the object.
