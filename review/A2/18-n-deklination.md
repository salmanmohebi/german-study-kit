---
title: N-Deklination
level: A2
topic_id: r-a2-18-n-deklination
tags: [a2, nomen, kasus]
---

- Ich kenne diesen {{c1::Studenten}} gut. | I know this student well. | Masculine nouns in *-ent* add **-en** in every case except the nominative singular.
- Wir haben mit dem {{c1::Kollegen}} gesprochen. | We spoke with the colleague. | *der Kollege → dem Kollegen* in the dative.
- Er hilft einem {{c1::Nachbarn}}. | He helps a neighbour. | *der Nachbar* is an n-noun too, despite ending in a consonant.
- Fragen Sie bitte den {{c1::Herrn}} dort. | Please ask the gentleman over there. | *Herr* is irregular: *den Herrn* singular, *die Herren* plural.
- Der {{c1::Junge}} spielt im Hof. | The boy is playing in the yard. | The **nominative** keeps the bare *-e* form: *der Junge*.
- Ich habe den {{c1::Jungen}} gesehen. | I saw the boy. | Accusative → *den Jungen*, with the added *-n*.
- Sie arbeitet als {{c1::Journalistin}}. | She works as a journalist. | Feminine forms in *-in* are **not** n-nouns and stay unchanged.
- Der Name des {{c1::Präsidenten}} steht im Brief. | The president's name is in the letter. | Genitive of an n-noun is *-en*, not *-s*.
- Wir laden alle {{c1::Kunden}} ein. | We're inviting all the customers. | *der Kunde → die Kunden*, plural identical to the singular oblique form.
- Er ist ein {{c1::Experte}} für Datenschutz. | He is an expert on data protection. | Nominative after *ein* stays *Experte*; accusative would be *einen Experten*.
- Ich vertraue diesem {{c1::Menschen}} nicht. | I don't trust this person. | *der Mensch* adds *-en* everywhere but the nominative singular.
- Der Bericht des {{c1::Polizisten}} war klar. | The policeman's report was clear. | Nouns in *-ist* follow the n-declension.
- Sie hat einen {{c1::Elefanten}} gesehen. | She saw an elephant. | Animals in *-ant* also decline this way: *der Elefant → den Elefanten*.
- Das Herz des {{c1::Patienten}} schlägt normal. | The patient's heart is beating normally. | *der Patient* is the classic medical n-noun.
